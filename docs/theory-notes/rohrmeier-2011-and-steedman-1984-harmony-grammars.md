# Harmony grammars: Rohrmeier (2011) and Steedman (1984)

Source PDFs: `docs/ref/Towards a generative syntax of tonal harmony.pdf` (*J. Mathematics
and Music* 5(1): 35–53) and `docs/ref/steedman1984.pdf` (*Music Perception* 2(1): 52–77,
"A generative grammar for jazz chord sequences"). Read: Rohrmeier's rule sets (pp. 39–43);
Steedman's introduction and rules 0–5 (book pp. 61–66; rules 1, 2 and 5 checked against the
rendered pages). Not read: Rohrmeier's analyses
and discussion; Steedman's extensions, the corpus examples and the diminished-chord rules.

## Rohrmeier: four levels, context-free
Levels: phrase → functional (regions TR/SR/DR; terms t, s, d, tp, sp, dp, tcp) → scale
degree → surface chord. Key is carried down the tree.

**Functional expansion rules** (TR = tonic region, SR = subdominant, DR = dominant):
- TR → DR t (dominant regions prepare tonics)
- DR → SR d (predominants prepare dominants)
- TR → TR DR (a phrase can end on the dominant: half cadence) — and XR → XR XR (any
  region may repeat/prolong itself)
- TR → t, DR → d, SR → s
- A phrase ending on V (half cadence) and a plagal cadence are generated by additional
  rules; a lone dominant with no tonic reference cannot be generated.

**Substitution (parallels):** t → tp (vi in major: deceptive cadence), s → sp (ii),
d → dp (rare, minor), t → tcp (counter-parallel).

**Modulation:** any non-tonic function may become a local tonic: key change is just
re-rooting, so a tonicisation and a modulation differ only in how high in the tree
and how long. Mode change rule gives borrowing (minor iv in major, etc.).

**Scale-degree level:** secondary dominants X → V/X X, and diatonic descending fifth
chains (e.g. "Autumn Leaves" style). Interface: t → I | I IV I; s → IV; d → V | VII;
tp → VI (major) / III (minor); sp → II.

The paper's claim: harmonic progressions exceed Markov transition tables; hierarchy
and recursion are needed. He explicitly notes pop and jazz phrases may not share one
overarching tonic, hence the separate phrase level.

## Steedman: blues as time-preserving rewrite rules
- Rule 0: 12-bar frame `I7 IV I V7 I` … each chord holding 2 bars.
- Rule 1: x → x x (each half as long). Rule 2: x → x Sd(x) (add the subdominant).
- Rule 3: any chord w before x7 can be replaced by D(x)7 x7 (insert a dominant seventh
  before a dominant seventh target; extends an authentic cadence backward, recursively).
- Rule 4: D(x)7 x → ♭St(x)7 x (tritone/flat-supertonic substitution).
- Rule 5: x x x → x, St(x)m, M(x)m: three copies of a chord become the chord, then the
  minor chord on its supertonic, then the minor chord on its mediant (I I I → I IIm IIIm;
  "widely used in jazz"; applies at other metric levels too).
- **Duration rule:** the right-hand side occupies the same total time as the left. Rules
  split a chord into shorter chords without changing the cycle length.
- Restrictions: Rule 2's subdominant substitution works on the metrically weak
  (right-hand) branch of the metric tree, not the strong (left) one. Replacing bar 1 of
  a blues by IV gives a sequence that is no longer a blues. Rule 3's `w` may not match a
  dominant seventh introduced by an earlier rule or a chord whose root was already changed
  by a substitution, to stop endless recursion.

## What it means for us
1. **Both grammars are time-preserving expansion.** That matches our harmonic-rhythm
   constraint (preserve cycle length and order). The grammar gives a *principled* way
   to do what `harmonicRhythm.js` does by moving whole bars: split a slot in two
   (Rule 1/2, on the weak half) and fill the new slot by function (a predominant before the dominant,
   a dominant before the tonic), instead of just changing durations.
2. **Where it applies.** Rohrmeier's directional rules fit common-practice, jazz and
   lo-fi jazz-ish presets; de Clercq–Temperley says pop/rock is symmetric. So pick the
   model per preset family (add a `harmonicStyle` profile: `functional` | `modal-loop`).
3. **A cadence generator, not a progression generator.** The most useful piece is the
   backward-extending authentic cadence: given a target (I at a section boundary),
   build `… ii V I` or `… vi ii V I` or `… V/V V I` by repeated rule application,
   bounded to 1–2 expansions. This replaces the fixed V–I in `sectionCadence.js` and
   gives `commonToneBridge.js` more candidates.
4. **Substitution table.** t→vi (deceptive), s→ii (sp), tritone sub ♭II7 for V7. All
   are low-risk colour for jazz-leaning presets (lo-fi, late-night, piano).
5. **Keep it bounded.** Rules must carry a recursion cap and restrict substitution
   (Steedman's `w` may not match an already substituted chord). A deterministic
   fallback to the plain progression keeps the planner safe.
6. **Modulation as tonicisation** gives a model for `keyLift` and drift: reinterpret a
   chord of the old key as the first chord of the new key (pivot) rather than jumping.
